Sports nutrition

Fuel your training. Protect your health.

For athletes, gym-goers and fitness trainers. Your dietitian builds a plan around your sport, your training week and your goal, and your doctor is involved when your health needs it.

Who it is for

Choose the group that sounds most like you. If you are between two, tell us when you book.

Endurance

Runners, cyclists, swimmers, triathletes

Long sessions that need steady fuel and fluids before, during and after.

Team sports

Football, basketball, cricket, hockey

Stop-start effort, match days and busy weeks with training, school or work.

Strength and power

Lifting, sprinting, throwing, CrossFit

Gym and general fitness

Gym-goers and fitness trainers

Fat loss, muscle gain or maintenance, matched to how often and how hard you train.

What your dietitian looks at

A sports plan only works when it fits your real week. We start with how you train and live.

1

Your sport and training week

Sessions a week, how long and how hard, double days, travel, competitions and when your season peaks.

2

Whether you are eating enough for your training

We compare what you eat with what your training uses. Eating too little for your training can cost you performance, bone health and hormones, in men as well as women.

3

Fuel around sessions

What to eat before, during and after training, matched to the length and intensity of each session.

4

Fluids and sweat

How much you sweat, how you feel in the heat, and a drinking plan that keeps you hydrated without overdoing it.

5

Blood results and health

Iron, haemoglobin and vitamin D are common gaps in athletes. Your doctor reviews results and treats any deficiency. We also ask about injuries, tiredness, illness and, for women, your periods.

6

Your goal and a pace that is safe

Fat loss, muscle gain, a weight class or simply performing better, with progress checked at every follow-up.

What you receive

A plan built for you

  • Daily targets that change with the day: rest, light, moderate, hard training and competition days
  • A fuelling plan for before, during and after sessions
  • A drinking plan based on your sweat and your sport
  • Meals and snacks that fit your culture, budget and schedule
  • Protein spread through the day to support recovery

For gym-goers and fitness trainers

  • A goal: lose fat, gain muscle or maintain
  • An energy target based on your whole training week
  • A realistic weekly pace, so weight change stays steady and you keep your strength
  • Protein guidance for your goal and experience
  • Follow-ups to adjust as your training changes

What to bring to your first visit

  • Your training schedule for a normal week
  • Your competition dates, if you have them
  • Recent blood test results, if you have them
  • A typical day of eating and drinking, including snacks and sports drinks
  • Any supplements or sports products you use
  • Your goal, in your own words

How we keep you safe

We take a food-first approach. Supplements are checked for safety and for your sport's anti-doping rules, and we do not support crash diets, rapid weight cuts or dehydration to make weight. If your screening shows signs that you are not eating enough for your training, your doctor is brought in.

Not for emergencies. If you feel faint, have chest pain or feel very unwell during exercise, stop and contact your local emergency services.

Common questions

Sports nutrition

Do I need to be a competitive athlete?

No. We work with weekend runners, gym-goers and fitness trainers as well as competitors. Tell us how you train and what you want to achieve.

Can you help me lose fat and keep my muscle?

Yes. Your dietitian sets a modest energy target for your training week, a steady pace of weight change and a protein plan, then checks your progress and strength at every follow-up.

Do I have to take supplements?

No. Most people do well on food first. If a supplement could help, your dietitian explains the benefits, the risks and whether it suits your sport's rules.

Can you help me make a weight class?

Yes, with enough time and a gradual plan. We do not support rapid weight loss or dehydration, because both harm performance and health.

Is the consultation online?

Yes. Consultations and follow-ups are online, so you can join from home or while travelling for competition.

How it works

What happens, step by step

Every patient is looked after in the same clear order, so nothing is skipped and your care always rests on a clear diagnosis.

1

We look at the whole picture

Your doctor and dietitian ask about your health, medicines, daily routine, food and drink, and review any test results or body-composition reports you share.

2

We explain what we found

Your doctor makes the medical diagnosis. Your dietitian names the nutrition problems that matter most. Both explain it in plain words, so you know what the plan is for.

3

You decide, with expert guidance

Your doctor advises on treatment. Your dietitian explains your options and the guardrails for your condition. You choose the dietary pattern that fits your life and culture, and we support it with therapy, reminders and follow-up.

4

We check progress together

At each follow-up your care team compares with where you started, celebrate what is working and change what is not.

What to bring to your first visit

  • Recent blood test or lab reports (PDF or photo)
  • Body-composition report, if you have one
  • A list of your medicines and supplements
  • A typical day of eating and drinking
  • Your questions and what you want to change

Your information

Your record is used for your care. Only the clinicians involved in your care can see your clinical record. Reports you upload are checked by your care team before anything is added to it.

Not for emergencies. If you feel very unwell, contact your local emergency services.

How a dietitian earns a specialty

Before seeing patients in a specialty, a dietitian goes through four stages. This is how we keep care consistent.

4. ReviewerChecks the work of colleagues in training.
